# Demand validation method (web)

Use this loop before coding. A search ranking, a star count, or a complaint is a
signal to inspect — not proof of demand, abandonment, or opportunity.

## The seven-step loop

1. **Seed from the owner's life.** List real exams, courses, languages, local
   knowledge, work, and problems the owner can personally verify. Never pick a
   niche only because an agent suggested it. The owner's advantage is usually:
   first-hand access to the source material, the local language, and the
   ability to check facts.
2. **Search the exact phrase.** Search what the user would type, in their
   language, for their country. Record query, country, language, date, and the
   URLs of the top five results — not just the first one.
3. **Score the five.** For each: does it load fast on a phone? Is it behind a
   login, an app, a paywall, or five ads? Is the content current? Is it usable
   offline? Does it work at 200% text size? Read the comments where users can
   leave them (YouTube comments, Reddit threads, Quora, forum posts, app-store
   reviews of the *app* version) and note recurring, verifiable complaints.
4. **Classify the space.** Crowded-but-bad can be an opportunity. An empty
   search usually means no demand. A giant-owned category (Wikipedia, a
   government portal, a big ed-tech brand) may be impossible to displace — say
   so instead of pretending otherwise.
5. **Find the niche inside the niche.** Choose a language, board, state,
   syllabus, or audience the owner can serve with uncopyable care:
   source-checked local detail, excellent language quality, offline access, and
   zero friction.
6. **Check incumbent friction honestly.** Open the candidates on a real phone.
   Note where they interrupt the task: forced app install, login wall, ads
   mid-scroll, cookie wall, broken mobile layout, wrong year's syllabus.
7. **Write the verdict line.** Format: `Demand: … · Freshness: … ·
   Complaints: … · Owner advantage: … · Decision: build / validate further /
   reject.` If evidence is missing, the verdict is "validate further", not
   "build now".

## Worked example (short, dated, and deliberately unflattering)

Query: `WB polytechnic 3rd sem cst previous year question paper`, West Bengal,
English, checked 2026-11-06.

- **What exists:** a mix of coaching-site PDF dumps, aggregator blogs with
  interstitials, and Telegram/WhatsApp groups. Several rank with pages that load
  several ad scripts before the first paragraph.
- **Observed:** results are often a single year, scanned as images (not
  searchable, not screen-reader readable), with no unit mapping, no answer key,
  and no offline copy. Some pages are updated for the wrong semester.
- **Not proven:** actual search volume, how many students want offline access,
  or whether anyone would pay. Do not infer those.
- **Owner advantage:** direct access to the current syllabus and question papers,
  plus the ability to verify each answer against the textbook — the one thing
  an aggregator cannot fake.
- **Verdict line:** `Demand: category clearly used, exact volume unmeasured ·
  Freshness: mixed, some pages stale for the current semester · Complaints:
  ad-heavy, image-only papers, no answers · Owner advantage: source-checked
  syllabus + answer keys + offline PWA · Decision: build a small offline-first
  paper+answer viewer, validate further with real classmates before adding more
  subjects.`

That is a template for the shape of the verdict, not evidence for a new site.
Every number above must be re-verified (or removed) by the owner at the moment
of use.

## Anti-patterns

- **Agent-invented demand:** "students need this" without a source.
- **One hostile site = a market:** a single bad incumbent proves one bad site.
- **Volume theatre:** keyword-tool scores and "100K searches" screenshots with
  no source, country, or date.
- **Copy-then-compete:** republishing another site's question bank, notes, or
  translations. Illegal, and the only durable advantage is the source-checking.
- **Scope by ambition:** ten subjects before one subject works offline.
