  1. 1

    Start here · Unit 2 · Linear Data Structures

    Trace stacks and circular queues

    Quick win

    Why here: Push/pop, postfix and circular queues recur in full questions. One trace table helps with algorithms, boundary cases and objective items.

    Read first

    • Write stack operations and evaluate postfix
    • Trace front and rear in a circular queue

    Try it: Evaluate the postfix expression 8 3 − 2 × with a stack. Then use a circular capacity of four and trace rear as it wraps after a dequeue.

    Past-paper practice

    • January 2024 · Q2 · 8 marks total Write push and pop algorithms with overflow and underflow checks.
    • March 2023 · Q4 · 8 marks total Write circular-queue insertion and deletion algorithms.
    Unit notes Practice questions
  2. 2

    Unit 3 · Linked Lists

    Draw before and after each list operation

    Build on the basics

    Why here: Insertion and comparison questions reuse a before/after diagram. Practise singly linked lists first, then add back links or a circular tail.

    Read first

    • Insert and delete at the head, tail and middle
    • Draw singly, doubly and circular variants

    Try it: On paper, insert into an empty list, insert in the middle, and delete the last node. Label which references change each time.

    Past-paper practice

    • December 2024 · Q7 · 8 marks total Insert a node at the beginning of a singly linked list.
    • January 2026 · Q6 · 8 marks total Compare a linked list with an array and describe the advantage of back links.
    Unit notes Practice questions

    Unit 1 · Introduction to Data Structures

    Name the structure and its operations

    Quick win

    Why here: The definitions are fast to revise, but most of the practice here is comparing and explaining. Put the reusable stack and linked-list algorithms first unless the terms are unfamiliar.

    Read first

    • Linear versus non-linear structure with examples
    • Basic operations and abstract data type

    Try it: Pick a linear structure and a non-linear one. Sketch both and describe what it means to visit every element in each.

    Past-paper practice

    • June 2022 · Q5(a) · 6 marks total Define a data structure and compare linear with non-linear structures.
    • December 2024 · Q3 · 8 marks total Give an example of an abstract data type.
    Unit notes Practice questions
  4. 4

    Unit 4 · Non-Linear Data Structures

    Separate tree and graph traces

    Longer practice

    Why here: Trees and graph traversals have good past-paper coverage, but they need several distinct traces. Give AVL, traversal and graph work separate time.

    Read first

    • Reconstruct trees and practise AVL rotations
    • Trace BFS/DFS with a named start vertex

    Try it: Take a small tree and write all three traversal orders. On a small graph, run BFS and DFS from the same vertex and compare the orders.

    Past-paper practice

    • June 2022 · Q6(c) · 6 marks total Reconstruct the tree from in-order and pre-order and give post-order.
    • January 2026 · Q7 · 8 marks total Explain AVL rotations and construct an AVL tree from the given values.
    Unit notes Practice questions

Last-minute checklist

Close your notes and check that you can do each of these.

  • Check empty and full conditions in a stack or queue trace.
  • Draw a linked-list insertion and label the updated links.
  • Redo a tree traversal and an AVL rotation without notes.
  • State the starting vertex and neighbour order when tracing a graph.
