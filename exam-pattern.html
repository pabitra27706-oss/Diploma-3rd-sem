Marks reconciliation — the printed maximum is supported

Every archived paper states its own set/answer quotas. Summing each group's answer-any quota or choice set gives the marks a candidate can actually score. That figure equals the printed maximum in 28 of 28 papers. The apparent conflict was a comparison error: the marks summed over all set questions are larger than the maximum because only a subset is answered.

What a mark value asks for

Sourced from the instructions printed on the papers themselves and from the syllabus table:

  • 1 mark: one objective or very short item. The 2022 syllabus pattern sets 15 and takes 10 in each of three sections.
  • 2 marks: a short answer. On the January-2024 to January-2026 papers each of the ten MCQs carries 2 marks with the printed split "1 mark for the right answer and 1 mark for the correct explanation".
  • 6 marks: a subjective question, answer any one of three alternatives per set, in the 2022 pattern.
  • 8 marks: a full subjective question, answer any five of nine, on the January-2024 to January-2026 papers.

No marking scheme for a 6- or 8-mark answer (point breakdown, word count) is published in the syllabus or on the papers, so none is invented here.

What stays unverified

  • Which pattern applies to your examination session: the syllabus table, or the shape printed on 28 archived papers. Ask your institution; the data does not decide it.
  • Whether any model answer matches an official key: no official key was supplied for any paper.
  • Whether a paper maximum belongs to a different regulation: the archived papers print no regulation label, and the field is recorded as unknown rather than guessed.
